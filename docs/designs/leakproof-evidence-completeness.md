## Problem Statement

Indian marketplace sellers (Amazon, Flipkart) lose a material share of settlement
value inside reports nobody reads: commission overcharges, fee errors, weight
overcharges, returns that never physically return, refunds whose fees are never
reversed, unpaid orders, tax mismatches, and unexplained deductions — compounded
by claim windows that expire unfiled. Existing tools flag discrepancies; none
tell the seller what is actually collectable, through which mechanism, by which
deadline, with which evidence — and none publish measured detection accuracy.
LeakProof is a Razorpay Buildathon Track 04 (AI Finance Controller) submission
that closes that loop, judged against a rubric rewarding explainable, bounded,
human-gated agents with honest metrics.

## Demand Evidence

Honest state: **third-party corroboration only, zero first-party evidence.**

- Headline claim: **2–5% of marketplace settlement value** (Terra Insight).
  Adjacent estimates use different bases and are cited separately, not as the
  same number: 2–3% of gross payment volume (reconciliation-industry sources),
  3–5% of GMV (Osfin, Revalsys). Citation trail kept in the repo.
- Category willingness-to-pay validated by incumbents: LogiRecon, Ecommatrics,
  TrackVid, Unicommerce, Threecolts, GoNukkad all charge money for weaker loops.
- No seller has been interviewed, named, or committed. Acceptable for a judged
  buildathon; the first gap to close if this outlives the hackathon.

## Status Quo

Manual reconciliation in Excel, 10–15 hours/week — and most sellers simply don't
do it. Alternatives: CA-led services, or dashboard-and-flag SaaS that stops at
flagging. The demo's real competitor is the boring spreadsheet.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

A seller operating on Amazon + Flipkart with roughly 100–5,000 orders/month,
doing recon in Excel or not at all. **No actual human is named yet** — open
question; the assignment starts closing it.

Wedge: upload one batch (orders + settlement reports + returns report + bank
statement, plus an optional catalog-dims CSV for weight audits) → reconciled
ledger + an exception queue where every exception
carries a computed evidence state — claim-ready, blocked (named blocker), not
claimable (named rule), or unexplained (bounded confidence) — with drafted,
human-gated claims for the ready ones. No integration, no marketplace logins,
no standing infrastructure. The bank leg reconciles at **payout level** (
settlement payout total ↔ bank UTR credit), not order level; order↔settlement
remains the row-level match.

## Premises (agreed in session)

1. **P1 — Schema is public; pathology is scarce.** Amazon Settlement Flat File V2
   is column-documented by SP-API and ≥5 commercial integrators (A2X, Openbridge,
   Intentwise, DataChannel, Celigo). Its shape is narrow (`amount-type` /
   `amount-description` / `amount`), not wide fee-per-column. Consequence: week 1
   is parsing, not archaeology; the real-seller ask retargets from "send me your
   file" to "send me one weird row that broke your spreadsheet."
2. **P2 — On Flipkart, detection ≠ claimability.** SPF eligibility appears to
   gate on active VMS packing video; a claim can be validly detected yet
   unwinnable because the deciding evidence is a video only the seller has.
   *Secondary-source lead (WareIQ, TrackVid); verification fallback in Resolved
   Decisions D14.* Consequence: the evidence model distinguishes
   report-derivable from seller-suppliable evidence.
3. **P3 — Encoded eligibility rules beat a third marketplace.** Example: SAFE-T
   excludes seller-issued refunds and A-to-Z transactions, so a naive
   refund-without-reversal detector false-positives on exactly the non-claimable
   cases. Consequence: Meesho and the Q&A chat are cut permanently (not "if
   slack appears" — week 4 has no slack).
   *Why Flipkart still earns its place under P3's own logic:* a single-
   marketplace tool cannot demonstrate the canonical-schema generalization
   claim, and the P2 constraint is itself demo material — but "drop Flipkart
   entirely" is a pre-committed descope rung, not an unthinkable.

## Approaches Considered

- **A — On-plan, descope pulled forward.** The original PDF plan with Meesho and
  Q&A cut day one. Strict subset of B; its day-one descope decision is retained
  inside B.
- **A′ — Amazon-only deep auditor.** One marketplace, no evidence model, all
  remaining time into detector depth and UI polish. Genuinely different bet:
  maximum reliability, minimum thesis. Rejected because it forfeits both
  differentiators (evidence-completeness and generalization); survives as the
  final descope rung.
- **C — Open rate-card corpus as the product.** Rejected as the central claim:
  publishing a dated fee rulebook makes corpus accuracy the product's
  reputation. Its best idea is kept — every rule carries a source URL and
  as-of date.

## Recommended Approach — B: Evidence-Completeness Engine

Central claim: not "we find leakage" (every incumbent's sentence) but **"we tell
you what you can actually recover, and exactly what blocks the rest."**

### Pipeline

ingest/normalize → three-way match (order → settlement line(s), exact joins
first, bounded fuzzy per D5; payout ↔ bank UTR per D6) → deduction audit against
versioned, dated, cited rate-card config → **deterministic exception
classification** (the class is assigned by which detector fired — never by the
LLM; D1) → **evidence-completeness computation (deterministic; D3)** → LLM
narration + claim drafting (prose only; D2) → human gate → claim pack (D8) →
reporting + append-only audit trail.

### Detected error classes (8 — also the seeded classes)

| # | Class | Detector basis | Mechanism | Source files |
|---|-------|----------------|-----------|--------------|
| 1 | Commission overcharge | rate-card % vs charged | SAFE-T / support ticket | settlement + rate card |
| 2 | Fixed/closing fee error | fee-slab lookup vs charged | support ticket | settlement + rate card |
| 3 | Weight/shipping overcharge | weight-slab + zone vs charged | SAFE-T / support ticket | settlement + **catalog dims (optional 5th input)** |
| 4 | Return marked returned, never received | returns report disposition vs restock/settlement | SAFE-T (AMZ) / SPF (FLP) | returns + settlement |
| 5 | Refund without fee reversal | refund event vs reversal lines | SAFE-T / support ticket | settlement |
| 6 | Unpaid order past cycle | delivered order absent from all batches **> 2 settlement cycles (~14 days) after delivery** | support ticket | orders + settlement |
| 7 | TCS/TDS mismatch | simplified Sec-52 TCS (1%) / 194-O TDS recompute vs reported | **CA review** (export bundle; no filing) | settlement + order values |
| 8 | Unexplained deduction | deduction with no rule basis | none (investigate) | settlement |

Mechanism enum: `SAFE-T | SPF | support-ticket | CA-review | none`. (GST filing
is out of scope; class 7 terminates in a CA-review export, per the original
plan's "detect mismatches, don't file.") Input-set notes: catalog dims ship as
an optional fifth CSV (the generator emits it); when absent, detector 3 is
disabled and reported as disabled. If the day-1 column check finds no
disposition/restock signal in a marketplace's returns report, detector 4 runs
in reduced two-file mode (returned-but-no-settlement-reversal) and says so.
Class 7 is deliberately simplified, never reaches CLAIM-READY by construction,
and is the first descope rung (rung 0).

### Evidence-state model (all computed, none LLM-generated)

Per exception:

```
mechanism:    from class table above
eligibility:  non-window rule checks with citations (A-to-Z exclusion,
              seller-issued-refund exclusion, ...) — window arithmetic is its
              own step below, not an eligibility rule
evidence[]:   { requirement,
                source: report-derivable | seller-suppliable | unobtainable,
                status: satisfied | missing | pending,
                source_line_ids: [..] }   # rows backing this item; populated
                                          # by the detector, consumed by D2(b)
                                          # and D8's cited-rows CSV
blocker_kind: seller-action | timing | professional-review   (BLOCKED only)
deadline:     computed per mechanism from return-scan/refund dates; null only
              for mechanism none/CA-review (no window exists); a window that
              exists but cannot be computed (source date missing) is NOT null —
              see step 3
```

**State derivation (ordered precedence — first match wins, so every exception
lands in exactly one state):**

0. Mechanism `none` (class 8, incl. unknown `amount-description` codes) →
   `UNEXPLAINED(basis)`, where basis is deterministic: `code-unseen` (value not
   in the fee vocabulary) or `code-known-no-rule` (known code, no rule basis
   for the amount). No confidence number; the basis label is the explanation.
1. Any eligibility rule fails → `NOT-CLAIMABLE(rule)`
2. Filing window expired → `NOT-CLAIMABLE(window expired)`
3. Window exists but source date missing/unparseable →
   `BLOCKED(timing, "window start date missing")`
4. Any required evidence has source `unobtainable` → `NOT-CLAIMABLE(evidence unobtainable)`
   (worked example: SPF claim type requiring a packing video for a seller
   known to have no VMS enrollment at order time — the video can never exist.
   Capability facts like VMS enrollment come from the optional seller-profile
   config, a sixth input the generator also emits: VMS enrollment window, GST
   registration, category set. Without that config this step is unreachable
   and such cases land at step 5 as
   `BLOCKED(seller-action, "<item> — permanence unknown")` — degraded
   honestly, never silently.)
5. Any required evidence `missing`/`pending` → `BLOCKED(blocker_kind, named item)`
   (e.g. "needs VMS packing video" / "awaiting settlement cycle 2" / "CA review")
6. Exception rests on a fuzzy match → `BLOCKED(seller-action, "confirm match")`
   — fuzzy-based exceptions never reach CLAIM-READY (D5)
7. Otherwise → `CLAIM-READY`

Queue sort: group by state first — CLAIM-READY, BLOCKED, UNEXPLAINED,
tax-review, NOT-CLAIMABLE — then within each group deadline ascending, null
deadlines last, ₹ descending. (Expired non-claimables never sit above
approvable claims.)

Reporting splits with an explicit additivity rule: **₹ identified = ₹
claim-ready + ₹ blocked** (classes 1–6 above materiality, disjoint states).
Disjoint from ₹ identified and from each other: **₹ unexplained** (class 8) /
**₹ tax-review** (class 7 — not marketplace-recoverable; terminates in a CA
export) / **₹ below-materiality** (aggregate, D3). The D5 fuzzy-basis figure
is an *annotation* (a labeled subset of ₹ blocked), never an additive seventh
line. Summing the four disjoint lines plus ₹ identified reproduces total
discrepancy value exactly. The demo beat where the agent refuses to draft an
unevidenced claim is the BLOCKED state rendering, not theater.

### Resolved Decisions (from adversarial review)

- **D1 Deterministic classification.** Exception class = which deterministic
  detector fired. The LLM never assigns class, mechanism, eligibility, or state;
  therefore no ₹ total is LLM-influenced.
- **D2 LLM numeral ban, implementable form.** LLM prose references amounts only
  as opaque placeholders (`{{amt:<line_id>}}`) rendered by the deterministic
  layer. Test, two parts: (a) no currency-formatted tokens in LLM-generated
  fields (order IDs, dates, cycle numbers are fine); (b) every `{{amt:...}}`
  must resolve to a line_id inside that exception's own evidence set —
  unresolved or foreign line_ids fail the render.
- **D3 Money representation.** Integer paise end-to-end. Comparison tolerance
  ±100 paise per rule (₹1). Materiality floor ₹10: below-floor discrepancies go
  to an aggregate "below-materiality" bucket — counted and reported, excluded
  from the exception queue and from headline ₹ identified.
- **D4 Unknown `amount-description` codes.** Mapped to "unclassified deduction";
  above floor → class-8 UNEXPLAINED exception; totals reported under
  ₹ unexplained, never silently dropped and never in ₹ identified.
- **D5 Fuzzy-match bounds.** A fuzzy order↔settlement join requires an exact
  match on at least one of {order ID, amount-in-paise}; edit-distance-1 on the
  ID alone is never sufficient. Tolerances when the other key flexes: amount
  within min(₹5, 0.5%), date within ±3 days. Every fuzzy match is flagged,
  counted separately in match rate, capped at BLOCKED (precedence step 6), its
  ₹ reported on a separate fuzzy-basis line, and the flag carried into the
  claim pack. Join performance: hash-join on candidate keys (exact-amount and
  date buckets), never a full cross-product — this is what keeps D13 honest.
- **D6 Bank leg granularity.** Payout-level only: settlement payout total ↔ bank
  UTR credit, from a single generic bank-CSV format (date, UTR, amount,
  narration) that the generator also synthesizes. Scheduled week 2 (small at
  payout level). Descope rung if needed.
- **D7 Quarantine denominator.** Malformed rows are quarantined with reasons,
  **remain in the match-rate denominator**, and the quarantine count is always
  displayed beside match rate.
- **D8 Approval artifact.** Approve produces a claim pack written to disk:
  clipboard-ready claim text + evidence bundle (cited source rows CSV +
  recomputation sheet) + audit-log entry. That is the terminal action; the
  no-network-side-effects test has this boundary.
- **D9 Metrics batches.** Measurement batch: 500 orders, 160 seeded errors
  (20/class), N=5 seeds, report mean ± range, per-marketplace and overall.
  Week 1's generator emits canonical + Amazon format only (bank CSV optional);
  Flipkart-format emission is a **week-2 deliverable gated on the same
  week-2 day-2 trigger as the parser** — until then the measurement batch is
  Amazon-format. Catalog dims and seller-profile config are emitted from week
  1. Demo batch for the video: 150 orders, 27 seeded errors. Framing: 160/500
  is a 32% *order-share* seeding rate, chosen for statistical power; the
  real-world 2–5% figure is a *value share* of settlements — different bases,
  and the README says so in one clause. If rung 4 (Amazon-only) is pulled, D9
  rebalances to Amazon-only: 8 classes × 20, or 7 × 23 if rung 0 is also
  pulled.
- **D10 Pass bars and measurement definitions.** Targets: overall recall
  ≥ 0.85, per-class recall ≥ 0.70, precision ≥ 0.95 (false positives burn
  seller credibility — precision beats recall at the drafting stage). Missing
  a bar triggers a descope rung; the measured number is published either way.
  Definitions: a true positive = a flagged exception whose (order/line id,
  class) matches a manifest entry; ₹-agreement is not required for TP but
  value-weighted recall is reported alongside. Seeded unknown-code deductions
  are manifest entries for class 8 and are scored; a class-8 flag on a clean
  row is a false positive — class 8 sits inside the precision denominator,
  not outside it. Out-of-corpus rows (D17) carry their own `UNCOVERED`
  disposition: excluded from the precision denominator, displayed as a count
  beside quarantine, and the measurement batch deliberately includes some so
  the corpus cap stays visible in published output. Generator invariant: every
  seeded error ≥ 2× the D3 materiality floor, so the floor can never eat a
  seeded error. Match rate = orders with complete order→settlement joins / all
  orders in batch, where class-6-flagged orders are **excluded from the
  denominator** and reported as their own line, so a correct finding never
  reads as a reconciliation failure; the payout leg counts toward "complete"
  only while the bank leg is in scope (rung 2 pulled → match rate is
  order↔settlement only, and says so).
- **D11 Reproducibility split.** `make verify` = deterministic: regenerates
  seeded batches and reproduces recall/precision/match-rate/₹ metrics with zero
  network calls. `make triage` = opt-in LLM job (narration, drafting, cost
  metrics), model + version recorded. Reproducibility claims apply only to the
  deterministic set.
- **D12 Anti-circularity of ground truth.** Manifest claimability labels are
  hand-authored against cited policy text **before the rule config is written**
  (labels in weeks 1–2, config after, with the labels file closed while coding
  the rules) — the ₹-claim-ready-vs-manifest metric exists to catch
  eligibility-rule bugs, and agreement-by-construction is the failure it must
  not have. The generator's fee logic and the detector's rate-card config are
  authored as two separate encodings of the same public sources. Additionally a
  small hand-authored adversarial holdout (cases the generator never produces)
  guards against correlated misunderstanding, and the README states plainly
  that figures are measured against seeded synthetic data. Holdout size: 25
  hand-authored cases; scored and published as its own line, never merged into
  headline recall/precision; no descope trigger attached — it is a disclosure,
  not a gate.
- **D13 Throughput target.** 10,000-order batch through the deterministic path
  in under 60s on a laptop; largest batch exercised before submission: 10,000.
  LLM spend only on exceptions (~tens of calls per batch).
- **D14 Policy-verification fallback.** SAFE-T/SPF/VMS rules encode this week
  from secondary sources, each rule carrying `verified: false` + source URL +
  as-of date, surfaced in UI and README. If live Seller Central / Seller Hub
  policy pages (login-walled) are not reached by end of week 1, the fallback
  stands automatically — unverified-but-labeled beats blocked.
- **D15 Donated-data handling.** Redaction checklist before any use (names,
  addresses, GSTIN, phone; order IDs hashed). Donated rows never enter the
  repo **or the pitch video** — only synthesized derivatives appear in public
  artifacts; the demo's "settlement screenshot" is generator-synthesized. LLM
  calls on real rows are opt-in per donor. Retention: local only, deleted on
  request; the solicitation message carries this promise.
- **D16 Demo hosting and keyless demo.** Locally-runnable only (`make demo`),
  unless the day-1 compliance check finds hosting mandatory. The repo ships
  pre-computed triage artifacts (narration + drafts) for the demo batch, so
  `make demo` is fully keyless end to end; `make triage` regenerates them with
  an API key, model + version recorded.
- **D17 Rate-card corpus, capped.** The versioned rate-card config is scheduled
  work, not an assumed input: week 1, capped at three Amazon categories + one
  Flipkart category, each rule dated and URL-cited. Detectors 1–3 fire only on
  categories the corpus covers; out-of-corpus rows take the explicit
  `UNCOVERED` disposition (own count beside quarantine, outside the precision
  denominator and outside every ₹ line) — never guessed, never class-8.
- **D18 Evaluation clock.** Every batch manifest carries an `as_of` date
  (default: the batch's max settlement date; overridable). All window
  arithmetic (precedence step 2) and the detector-6 cycle threshold evaluate
  against `as_of`, never the system clock — so `make verify` and the D16
  pre-computed demo artifacts reproduce identically at any later date.
- **D19 One exception per discrepancy.** Detectors run in class order 1→7;
  the first to fire claims the line_id, and class 8 fires only on lines no
  detector claimed. In detector 4's reduced two-file mode, class 5 wins the
  4-vs-5 overlap (fee-reversal evidence is the more specific basis). No ₹ is
  ever double-counted across classes.

### Descope ladder (pre-committed, in order)

Rule: when pulled *for schedule*, rungs are cumulative in listed order (pulling
rung N implies 0…N−1). A named trigger pulls **exactly the rung(s) it names**:
the week-2 day-2 Flipkart trigger pulls rung 4 alone (mooting rung 1; D9 goes
Amazon-only, 8 classes unless rung 0 is separately pulled); the
Razorpay-mandatory branch pulls rung 1 alone; the AI-mandatory branch reorders
rung 3 below rung 4.

0. Cut class 7 (TCS/TDS) — cheapest cut, zero thesis loss (it never reaches
   CLAIM-READY); D9 rebalances to 7 classes.
1. Flipkart SPF drafting → detection-only (evidence states still computed)
2. Drop bank-credit leg (keep order↔settlement two-way)
3. Replace LLM drafter with deterministic template drafter (narration cut) —
   moves below rung 4 if the day-1 check finds an AI component mandatory
4. Drop Flipkart entirely (→ approach A′) — auto-triggered by the week-2 day-2
   condition above
5. **Never dropped:** metrics harness, evidence-state model, audit trail —
   those are the submission.

### README limitations (verbatim, from the original plan + review)

- "₹ recovered" is really "₹ identified + claim-ready." Actual recovery depends
  on marketplace adjudication; filing is human-gated by design. Do not overclaim.
- Rate cards drift. Fees are versioned config, clearly dated; a production
  system would sync them continuously.
- Synthetic realism has limits. Real reports contain pathologies no generator
  anticipates; the one-real-seller run partially mitigates but does not
  eliminate this. All published accuracy figures are measured against seeded
  synthetic data.
- Eligibility rules marked `verified: false` were encoded from secondary
  sources with dates; treat their outputs accordingly.
- Hash-chained audit log is tamper-evident, not tamper-proof.
- Fuzzy matching is a judgment call; every fuzzy match is flagged and counted
  separately in the match rate.

## Success Criteria

- `make verify` reproduces recall / precision / match-rate / ₹ metrics from the
  repo with zero network calls; pass bars and measurement definitions per D10,
  published as measured.
- Every exception lands in exactly one state via the precedence ladder (steps
  0–7); every BLOCKED/NOT-CLAIMABLE names its blocker/rule on screen.
- The six ₹ lines (identified, claim-ready, blocked, unexplained, tax-review,
  below-materiality) reported separately everywhere (UI, README, video).
- Invariant tests pass: D2's two-part LLM output test; no network side effects
  beyond the opt-in LLM API; approve terminates in a claim pack (D8).
- Malformed-file demo shows quarantine reasons with the denominator handled per
